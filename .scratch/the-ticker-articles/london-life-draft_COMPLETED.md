Status: DRAFT for review. Nothing wired into `data/barometer.json` yet.
PROSE-REVIEW: all text below is new.

Scope: social barometer states (stable, unrest, lockdown, festival, crime). Headlines x2, deck, body, per state. Author hardcoded per section.

## Columnist: Pip Hollis

- **Byline:** `PIP HOLLIS · LONDON LIFE`
- **Author note (shown under byline):** *Pip has been at the Ticker eleven weeks and has opinions about all of it.*
- **Personality:** Junior reporter, sociable, unflappable. Files a disorder like a night out: queue lengths, who was serving, whether the chips were hot. Brave and oblivious in equal parts, so the danger is real but gets filed under "inconvenient". Never explains calc, assumes readers have some.
- **Register:** reported newspaper prose. Lede states what happened, second paragraph states the consequence for readers. Personality lives in word choice, quoted sources, and one dry detail per piece, not in anecdote.
- **Voice rules:**
  - Third person reporting. No "I", "my". Self-reference only as "this reporter" / "this newspaper", sparingly.
  - Recurring source: "a close personal source" (Pip's cousin, never named). Used for quotes and advice, at most once per piece.
  - Recurring details allowed (chip shop, the 38, buns, umbrella with a duck handle). Report them as fact, not as a diary.
  - Menace is reported, never denied; the tone just under-reacts to it.
  - One joke per beat. No exclamation marks, no addressing the player, no fourth wall (addresses "readers", the in-world audience).
  - Body copy is voice only. Mechanics live in the "WHAT CHANGES IN LONDON" panel.
  - Hint at the effect in-world (so the story and panel don't contradict), never quote numbers.

## Stable — "Normal levels of street activity"

Effects: none.

**Headlines**
1. Nothing happens in London; reporter checks twice
2. Quiet day on the streets as the 38 runs to time

**Deck:** A calm day citywide, described by this reporter as "suspicious in a nice way".

**Body:**
London was quiet today, a finding verified on foot between Bethnal Green and Waterloo. The 38 ran to time. A seller outside the Underground worked his patch at low volume, which observers class as manners. A bun bought at Waterloo was fine.

A close personal source advises that a calm week is for tidying stock, checking locks and doing the washing. The washing has been done. Readers carrying orichalcum or watching veins may wish to see to the other two, as experts are unclear on how long it will stay like this.

## Social Unrest — "Raid risks up, but so is opportunity"

Effects: mugging +8 pts, raid chance +10 pts.

**Headlines**
1. Unrest spreads between boroughs; police "elsewhere"
2. Bad night in Hackney, though the chip queue held up

**Deck:** With police attention stretched, the city is livelier than anyone asked for.

**Body:**
Unrest moved through several boroughs overnight, leaving broken windows and one bin found well outside its usual range. Police were largely elsewhere, a position a tired officer described as "operational" and a close personal source translated as "you're on your own, love". The chip shop on the high street stayed open throughout, to general respect.

With attention drawn away, anyone carrying valuables may be of interest to the crews who watch for nights like this. Locks are cheaper than losses. The same source notes that some regard a thin police presence as an opportunity.

## Lockdown — "Movement restricted. Searching harder, yields lower"

Effects: searching yields -15%, weekly living costs +10%.

**Headlines**
1. Lockdown notices go up on every lamppost
2. Everyone indoors, everyone fine, everyone asking about the rent

**Deck:** Restrictions on movement make everything slower and the bills a bit steeper.

**Body:**
Lockdown notices appeared overnight on lampposts across the city, several of them identical. The instruction is to stay in. One flat visited by this reporter has since established that it can hold a great many snacks.

The wider cost is less cheerful. With the streets shut, searching is slow and finds are smaller: a resident of one building reports four hours out and nothing but a meal deal to show for it. Costs indoors are rising too, from the landlord, the gas and the TV licence. Officials call the measures temporary, somehow while maintaining a staight face. Keep a biscuit in reserve.

## City Festival — "Physics ore in demand"

Effects: demand up for Blast, Shield, Black Hole, Wormhole; searching +5%.

**Headlines**
1. London throws a party, buys all the loud things
2. City Festival: stilts, flags, and a Wormhole with a ribbon

**Deck:** Festival crowds are buying the bangy, shiny and slightly alarming.

**Body:**
The City Festival filled the streets with flags and a man on stilts who is a genuine hazard to low-flying traffic. One stall sold a Wormhole, described as "the same as the one in the shop, but with a ribbon". A close personal source bought one. This reporter did not.

Demand is concentrated on Blasts, Shields, Black Holes and, yes, Wormholes, on the principle that nothing says celebration like something that goes off or keeps people away. Traders report brisk business and sore throats. Crowds are in good spirits and guarding their pockets less closely than usual, and anyone out searching for ore has had better luck than usual. Wear sensible shoes.

## Crime Wave — "Everyone is at it. Mugging risk elevated"

Effects: mugging +15 pts, home raid +5 pts.

**Headlines**
1. Crime wave: everyone is at it, reports Pip, who is not
2. A close personal source "knows a guy"; the guy is busy

**Deck:** Street theft and break-ins are both up, and nobody is sure who is not involved.

**Body:**
Six street robberies were reported to this newspaper today before the count was abandoned, partly from fatigue and partly because the reporter had been robbed too, of an umbrella with a duck handle. Its return would be welcomed.

Break-ins are rising alongside. A close personal source says homes are being done with the attention once given to weddings. Anyone with stock at home and no plan to guard it may find someone insistently offering to look after it instead. Readers carrying much of anything should consider where it sleeps. Lock the door, then check the lock.
