# Vein: Critical Review (build of 2026-10-09)

**Build:** branch `stage-engine` @ `e2075417`, plus the uncommitted working tree.
**Method:** a live playthrough in the Godot 4.7 editor at a 390×844 portrait canvas, covering New Game from the intro to the first vein handover, then Debug Start for a tour of every app and HQ zone. On top of that: two fights, the combat-balance sim (`scripts/sim_combat_balance.gd`, n=500), a read of all 75 event files, and the data tables and vision docs.
**Not verified:** taps on the map canvas. The input injector sends device −1 mouse events, which `map_canvas.gd:778` deliberately ignores, so that gap is the tool's fault, not the game's. I also didn't play the mid/late-game economy in person. The faction-economy sim covers it instead (60 days seed 1, and 120 days seed 7; see §6b).

Part 1 is written as a player with no access to the docs. Part 2 judges the build against `VISION.md`, `ui-vision.md` and `CONTENT-GUIDE.md`.

# PART 1 — AS A BLIND PLAYER

## 1. First impressions

The title screen is a grey void with "VEIN" in small body text and three pill buttons. For a game whose event art is this good, it's a wasted first frame: no logo, no art, no mood.

The sprite picker is functional but contextless. It's a fighter in a stance on grey, with no name and no explanation of what the choice affects. Then the intro hits, and it's a different game: a rain-slick Mile End alley in rich pixel art, a confident first line ("Rent is due Friday. You have £40…"), and Archie is instantly likeable. The Wetherspoons card with the chips half-eaten on the follow-up plate is a lovely touch.

## 2. Fun

**What's fun right now**
- Reading. Archie and James are funny, and James's "reasonably attentive puppy" speech is the first time I laughed.
- Discovering the phone. Each app is a little parody world (LodedInnit, Harrow's, The Ticker, Reynard's) and I wanted to poke them all.
- The lab bench. Picking a jar and tapping glassware to probe is a tactile, curious loop ("Inert. Nothing in it. Never was." is a great failure line).

**What isn't**
- **The first ~45 minutes are a visual novel with no choices.** Intro (18 cards), buyer (12), James (24), Archie chat (12), raid intro and debrief (15): about 80 taps of → before I make any decision. Only 17 of 75 events in the whole game contain a choice; the tutorial has none. The game keeps telling me I'm a participant ("You come along, you get a cut") while giving me nothing to do.
- **Time passes by pressing Rest.** "Wait for Archie's text" means opening HQ and tapping "Rest — next morning". Nothing tells you that. I skipped day 2 entirely with zero agency, which undercuts the "3 time blocks" fantasy before it starts.
- **Combat has no tension.** I won the first fight by tapping Attack. The sim confirms it's not luck (§6).
- **No stakes on the opening hook.** "Rent is due Friday" is the inciting tension, but bills land on Mondays, and at the bedsit missing rent just accrues interest: the forced downgrade is a no-op at the bottom tier. The threat that opens the game can't hurt you.

## 3. Onboarding & clarity

1. **"Vein" is never introduced before it's given to you.** The word doesn't appear in any tutorial card before the raid debrief, where Archie says "I said I'd give it to you and I meant it." He never said it on screen. I was handed my core economic asset with no idea what it was.
2. **The objective list contradicts the screen.** ToDo shows "Wait for Archie's text" with no way to act, while the objective data says "Back up Archie on the sale tonight. Check Contacts."
3. **All twelve phone apps appear on day 1**, most of them empty (Factions shows 0% everywhere, the BizBrief pot is "not open yet", Reynard's has no transactions). A new player gets a wall of apps and no pointer to the one that matters. Messages carries the badge but is buried in the dock.
4. **The Map is locked behind a greyed nav item** with the ticker saying "Stick close for now". That's fine, but nothing announces when it opens.
5. **The HQ diorama has no visible hotspots** apart from the bed caption. The desk, safe, noticeboard and dumbbells are all tappable, and you have to guess.
6. **The Dial is unexplained jargon at first contact:** "Level 1 Dial — Malacca Cane · Charge 0/0 · No Movement seated — the Dial is inert · Load Complication". Three new nouns, none defined.

## 4. UI / UX

**Strong**
- The phone shell is convincing, and each app has its own brand: Harrow's green-and-gold estate-agent listings, The Ticker's broadsheet serif, LodedInnit's plum.
- The departure-board top bar is a great London signature.
- The Map's Tube-diagram look and the Network Reference legend copy ("The lines are money. The dots are where it's coming from — or where someone beat you to it.") are excellent.
- The HQ lab bench and the recipe/experiments notebooks are a joy.

## 5. Gameplay loop (as experienced)

What I could piece together as a player: once tutorial gating lifts, each day has three blocks. You tend veins on the Map (cultivate, prune), craft at the HQ lab, sell through Archie (50/50) or faction shops via the Trade sheet, take BizBrief contracts, hire staff on LodedInnit, upgrade rooms and property on Harrow's, and handle raids and alarms. The Ticker news shifts demand.

That is a coherent, appealing management loop, and on paper it's exactly the game the pitch promises. The problem is **density without hierarchy**: by Debug Start there are 14 apps, three HQ zones with sub-sheets, a Map drawer with five filters, a Dial with Movements/Complications/Charge, guard kits, intel products, gifts, favours, peace talks, poaching, shares tables. Nothing in the UI says "this is the thing to do today". BizBrief's Brief tab ("Needs your attention") is the closest and should be the spine of every session.

**Economy feel (from the numbers):** a fair-terroir light prune yields roughly 25 ore (9 growth × 2.8), which is about £190 at the £75/10 London quote, or about £95 after Archie's half. Bedsit rent is £350/week. That's 3–4 prunes a week just to stand still, which is a reasonable early squeeze. Property then jumps to £80k for a studio, which needs a very different income tier. I couldn't verify the mid-game curve in play.

## 6. Mechanics

**Combat (3.5/10).** The presentation is good: the turn-order strip, the Dial art, and punch/KO keyposes with a decent hit-react. The rules underneath are thin:
- **The combat sim** (itemless player, always Attack) wins **100%** of every matched fight from L1 to L5, with mean end-HP 31–62%. The only loss case is the L1 street mugging (36%). Items and the Dial are optional for nearly all content.
- **Blast T3 dealt 8 damage, the same as a basic punch** (`effectPower` T3 = 8), and it costs 5 physics ore (about £35). T5 is 12. Damage items are strictly worse than attacking at most tiers.
- **Every enemy's intent is "Attacking".** There's nothing to read or answer.
- **The Time Pearl freeze has no visual state** on the frozen sprite, and its feedback arrives a turn late via the marquee.
- **"No way out of this one"** on raids removes Leg it as a choice, so the only decision is which button deals damage.

**Veins and cultivation.** The single signed growth axis (Barren↔Rampant, drifting from neutral) is elegant, and better than most idle-farm systems. The tutorial explanation is muddled, though (§7). Level caps by terroir give sites real value.

**Crafting and discovery.** The jar × gear probe grid is the most "game-like" system in the build: curiosity, a cost, a reveal. But a 40% base success rate on the very first recipe will feel punishing to new players.

**Market / Ticker.** Barometer-driven demand ("Conflict abroad: Blast demand +60%") feeding real prices is good design and well presented. It's also currently the *only* reason to craft Blast, given the combat numbers.

**Systems breadth.** 26.7k lines across about 70 system files: faction AI with stances, wars, truces, Conclave positions, intel meters, disinformation, poaching, gifts with diminishing returns, partner trouble asks, guard upkeep and short-pay, shares. It's impressive engineering and well tested. It is also far more simulation than a player can perceive through a phone UI. I saw almost none of it surface as a decision I understood.

## 6b. Economy simulation (`scripts/sim_faction_economy.gd`)

Two runs: 60 days on seed 1, and 120 days on seed 7. Both use the defaults: the player is credited 25 life ore a day, and faction rivalry is on.

| Finding | Evidence | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **The world is static** | Vein churn is `{}` for all five factions over 120 days. Vein counts never move (8/9/9/5/11). | Wars, raids and escalation produce headlines, but the map never changes hands. Pillar 2 needs territory to move. |
| **Runaway faction wealth** | Day 120: Guild **£1.18M**, Network £794k, Collective £185k, Firm £183k, Conclave **£75k**. The Guild is growing about £10k a day with no sink. | The "oldest, richest firm in London" is the poorest. Faction wallets need upkeep and war costs that scale with size. |
| **Relations drift linearly toward hostility** | col–net goes −1 a day steadily, from 0 to −41 by day 51. Every non-allied pair slides at a constant rate. | Wars are inevitable and on a timer rather than caused by events. gld–ccl fights 3× in 120 days. |
| **Wars can stall** | col–net war at day 121: Collective weariness 100/100, and the war is still running. | A maxed-weariness war should force talks or a collapse. |
| **Factions never go short** | `short {}` for every faction on every day. | Shortage-driven behaviours (steal, stockpile raid, buy pressure) never trigger, so a whole branch of FactionAI is dead in practice. |
| **The player is invisible in the market** | 25 ore a day = 7% life share, 0% elsewhere. Factions harvest 1,650–1,870 a day in total. | Market-moving fantasies ("crash a market you've stockpiled against") are out of reach until the player's scale is closer to a faction's. |
| **Price bands are narrow, with trends that never self-correct** | Ores stay at 0.57–1.32× base. Time ore slides to 0.64× by day 120 on Guild oversupply (+221 a day net). The four Pan's-prank items sit pinned at 1.17–1.26× all run. | Prices are mostly flat, which blunts The Ticker. Persistent oversupply should push producers to cut back. |
| **Independents dominate crafting** | 37–71% craft share in every ore. | Factions are minor crafters, so faction moves barely shift item prices. |
| **Player relations stay at 0** with no actions | `col 0 frm −1 gld 0 net 0 ccl 0` throughout. | Fine as a baseline, but there's no ambient pressure on a player who ignores factions. |

**Suggested sim targets** (proposal, not decided):
- At least 2–4 vein ownership changes per 60 days, across factions.
- The richest-to-poorest faction wallet ratio stays ≤3× at day 120.
- At least 1 faction shortage episode per 30 days.
- No war outlives weariness 100 by more than 3 days.
- Ore prices touch 0.6× or 1.5× at least once per 60 days.

## 7. Writing (7.5/10)

**Strengths.** The voices are distinct and consistent:
- **Archie:** blunt, warm and evasive ("One vein isn't a business, it's a hobby with rent.").
- **James:** contempt as a love language; the *Critias* aside lands.
- **Des:** decent, with history.
- **Hakim's yard** is the best scene in the game. Half-done homework on the counter, "It does about a third of what it used to. Which is about a third of the rent, so." It's restrained and sad, exactly the menace-and-comedy register.
- **The Firm intimidation** ("He hasn't threatened you. He's been very careful not to.") is excellent.
- **Harrow's listings** are consistently funny ("Viewing essential, as the photographer could not fit the room in.").

**Continuity errors (fix first).** The opening sequence contradicts itself at almost every step:

It also leans on "veins are a bit like gardens", which collides with the house rule that staff cultivate veins, not plants. The LodedInnit bios do the same ("discreet horticulture", "grower").

**Flavour deck is thin.** 19 district events, 13 of them with no choice, several 1–2 cards long ("foxes", "rain"). They're nicely written vignettes, but they don't create decisions. "soho_tout" teases a Soho market that the game doesn't have.

## 8. Art & audio

- **Event plates** (alley, Shoreditch, Wetherspoons, Bermondsey, James's study, Archie at the market) are excellent: consistent palette, readable silhouettes, strong lighting. The weaknesses are long holds (James's plate covers 15+ cards, including Archie's exit) and art/text mismatches (above).
- **The HQ dioramas** (bedsit, upgraded flat) are cosy and detailed. They're the emotional anchor of progression and should get more interaction.
- **Combat sprites** are crisp with good keyposes, but the stage backdrop is a flat gradient. The upgrade from bedsit to a real location backdrop matters more than more animation.
- **Phone icons** are clean and on-brand.
- **Audio:** I heard none. No music, no SFX, no tap feedback. For a game this atmospheric, sound (rain, Tube rumble, departure-board clack, a pint set down) is a cheap, enormous multiplier.

# PART 2 — AGAINST THE VISION DOCS

## 9. Design pillars (VISION §2)

| Pillar | Score | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Business Is the Game | **7 / 10** | Strongest pillar. BizBrief contracts, production targets, staff/payroll, the London market with barometer demand, and faction shops all exist and interlock. The gap is legibility, not substance. |
| 2. London Is a Place | **5 / 10** | The Map looks right and districts have ore bias. But **travel is free** (`travel.gd`: "District travel (free)"), which removes the routing decisions the pillar exists for. Ancient-London seeds (roman_brick, conclave_watch) are good but sparse. |
| 3. Everything Is Priced | **5 / 10** | Time blocks, cash, ore and relations are priced. **"Violence has an invoice: every fight opens with pay, talk, or intimidate"** isn't built: there's no Talk, Bribe or Intimidate anywhere (0 hits in `systems/`, `data/`). |
| 4. Menace and Comedy | **5 / 10** | Comedy delivered. Menace mostly isn't: 100% Attack-only win rates, no failure state at the bedsit, raids you win by tapping. "If a threat can't hurt you, it doesn't ship" is currently violated by most threats. |

## 10. Core loops & progression (VISION §3, §3b)

- **Session loop** (3 blocks → event → end day → tick): built. But the tutorial teaches it as "press Rest", and blocks don't feel scarce yet.
- **Prey → Operator → Problem:** the vision says power should be about 70% preparation (arsenal, equipment, allies, information, reputation) and about 30% light stats. The build inverts that. `combatSkill` scales HP, ATK and speed (`combatHpBonusByLevel` jumps by +55 and +140), while the arsenal is weak (Blast ≈ punch). **Equipment, reputation, affinities and Fieldcraft are absent.** Allies exist (recruit picker in combat prep).
- **"Enemies never scale to the player":** consistent with the data. But since even T1 enemies don't threaten an Attack-only player, growth isn't legible. The "Mugging Revisited" payoff has nothing to contrast against.

## 11. Combat 2.0 (VISION §11): **2 / 10 vs spec**

| Spec | Built? |
|---|---|
| Opener: Talk / Bribe / Intimidate / Fight | **No.** The prep screen offers Change loadout and Fight; Leg it is in-fight. |
| `reputation` stat | **No.** |
| Intent telegraphs (Swing/Heavy/Grab/Brace/Call/Bolt) | **No.** Enemies have no intent tables; the UI always shows "Intent: Attacking". |
| Consumables as *answers* to intents | **No.** With no intents, items are stat food, which is the exact failure the spec names. |
| Squad combat, turn queue, targeting | **Yes**, and well implemented (R§3.7a). |
| Short (3–6 turns), rare | Length about right; frequency unverified. |

This is the single largest vision gap, and it's the one that would most fix "Fun". The intent system is what turns crafting into tactics and gives the economy a reason to make anything but money.

## 12. Events framework (VISION §12)

- Data-driven events, auto-discovered art, the VN layout, the Item button (Rewind): **built, and solid.**
- **Choices with checks:** the schema supports them, but content barely uses them (17/75 events).
- **District deck "weighted by district, barometer, and flags" as the main exploration vehicle:** built, but with 19 mostly flavour-only cards. It needs 3–4× the volume, and most cards should carry a decision or a cost.
- **Item hooks beyond Rewind** (Luck, Prophet's Breath, Pan's Prank, Shield in events): not seen.

## 13. Economy, factions, endgame (VISION §13–15)

- **Selling lanes:** Archie → faction shops → contracts, built. The Soho Marketplace is replaced by the London market and Ticker, which is a reasonable substitution, but the "soho_tout" event still promises it.
- **Factions:** five exist with deep AI. Only the **Collective** has authored storyline content (Acts 1–2). Firm, Guild, Network and Conclave have key members and mechanics but not the "3-event storyline at 25/50/80" per faction.
- **The Conclave as lore channel:** a single deck vignette. The Deep Vein endgame (§15) isn't started; no data or system references it.
- **Equipment** (5–7 weapons/wearables): absent.

## 14. UI Vision: four families (ui-vision.md): **6.5 / 10**

| Family | State |
|---|---|
| 1. Physical-world pixel art (events, HQ, combat stage, lab) | **Excellent** where it exists. The combat stage backdrop is the gap. |
| 2. Phone-OS chrome | **Strong.** The diegetic home, branded apps and badges work. Static clock/date breaks the illusion. |
| 3. Tube-diagram chrome (Map) | **Good:** legend, drawer and palette all on-brand. Taps not verified (tool limitation). Your own playtest notes report dead taps after extended use. |
| 4. Field-kit HUD (combat) | **Incomplete:** the Dial art is great, but the cards, blank cream band, clipped text, white oval placeholder and marquee-as-log don't yet read as one designed HUD. |

## 15. Tone bible (VISION §17, CONTENT-GUIDE): **7 / 10**

- "One dry line per threat, not three": mostly respected.
- "If it winks, it dies": a few winks survive. "Mr Bean vibe", "Vinnie Jones in his prime", Usain Bolt, the Millennium Falcon keyring. Pop-culture references are the closest the script gets to winking.
- "Danger is sincere": the prose sells danger (the Stanley knife, the Firm), but the systems then make it toothless, so the tone and the mechanics are out of sync.
- James quoting Plato: once. The bible says "constantly". He could do it more, briefly.

## 16. Scope & production

- **1,304 commits, about 48k lines of GDScript, 189 test files, a strict data/state/system/screen architecture.** Engineering discipline is unusually high for a solo game. Rewind/snapshot purity and the data-driven content are real assets.
- **The risk is breadth.** VISION §2 says the pillars are "the scope filter". The build has grown intel markets, disinformation, poaching, gifts, favours, truce payments and guard short-pay, while the M2 Combat 2.0 spec, equipment and endgame remain unbuilt. A lot of effort is invested in simulation the player can't see, and too little in the moment-to-moment decisions the player can.
- **Toolchain mismatch:** `CLAUDE.md` targets Godot 4.7, but `godot` on PATH is **4.4.1**, and the README's Android steps reference 4.4 export templates. The `_mcp_game_helper` autoload fails to parse under 4.4 (`Logger` type missing) on every headless run. Pin one version.
- **Test suite:** see the result appended at the end.

# PART 3 — PROPOSALS

## 18. P1: Opening-hour rewrite (choices + rolls)

### 18.1 What the event engine can do today

- A `choice` card holds options. Each has fixed `result_text` and `effects`, and the event stays linear: it continues to the next card whatever you pick (`events.gd:226`).
- A `chance` effect op exists, with flat `p` and `on_success`/`on_fail` effects (`events.gd:335`). However, **result text can't branch on the roll**, and the odds are invisible to the player.
- There's no conditional card, no branching jump (`goto`), and no way to show odds or modifiers.

### 18.2 Proposed schema extension: a "check" on a choice

```jsonc
{
  "type": "choice",
  "text": "Marcus turns the calc over. 'Eighty.'",
  "choices": [
    { "label": "Take it", "result_text": "...", "effects": [ { "op": "add", "path": "player.cash", "value": 40 } ] },
    {
      "label": "Push for more",
      "requires": null,                       // optional gate: flag / item / cash / contact relation
      "check": {
        "base": 0.40,
        "mods": [
          { "flag": "intro_askedQuestions", "add": 0.15, "label": "You asked the right questions" },
          { "skill": "sales", "perLevel": 0.05, "label": "Sales" },
          { "item": "prophetsBreath", "add": 0.25, "consume": true, "optional": true, "label": "Prophet's Breath" },
          { "relation": "archie", "atLeast": 15, "add": 0.10, "label": "Archie backs you" }
        ],
        "min": 0.05, "max": 0.95,
        "show": "odds"                         // "odds" | "hint" (Likely/Risky) | "hidden"
      },
      "success": { "result_text": "...", "effects": [ { "op": "add", "path": "player.cash", "value": 60 } ] },
      "fail":    { "result_text": "...", "effects": [ { "op": "add", "path": "player.cash", "value": 30 }, { "op": "relation", "contact": "archie", "value": -3 } ], "goto": 9 }
    }
  ]
}
```

**Rules:**
- **Odds come from data.** Add `base`, then each matching mod; clamp to `[min, max]`. The button shows the result: **"Push for more · 60%"**. Tapping a small ⓘ lists the mods that apply ("+15% You asked the right questions").
- **Optional items** (`optional: true`) show as a toggle under the choice ("Use Prophet's Breath? +25%"). The item is only consumed if it's toggled on.
- **Rolls are fixed per event.** Seed each roll from (event id, card index, choice index, game seed) through `Rng`. Rewinding and re-picking the *same* option with the *same* mods gives the same result, so Rewind can't be used to fish for rolls. Changing the inputs (using an item, picking a different option) changes the outcome. Using Rewind as "undo my choice" still works.
- **Choices are recorded** in a dedicated store, `flags.choices.<eventId>.<cardIndex> = "<option-id>"`, so later events can read them as `flag` mods or `requires`.
- **`goto`** (an optional card index) allows short branches. Keep them shallow: rejoin within 2–4 cards.
- **`requires`** hides an option, or shows it disabled with a reason ("Needs £50"), so prepared players see extra routes.
- **Engine work:** `Events.choose()` resolves the check and stores `{text, success}` in `choiceResults`. `events.gd` gains `_check_odds(check) -> {p, mods}`, which is pure and testable. The VN card renders the percentage and the ⓘ sheet. The quest editors (`tools/quest-editor*.html`) need the new fields.

### 18.3 Time continuity (lands with P1)

- **Event timing in data.** Each event gets an optional `"at": { "block": "evening" }`. Starting the event sets the world block, so the board, card header and art agree. "Tonight" events run in the Evening block, and "tomorrow midday" means next day, Afternoon.
- **Generated card headers.** Headers like "Earlier tonight" or "Two days later" become generated (`{block}`/`{dayRelative}` tokens) or are checked against `at` by a content test.
- **Phone clock.** The phone's status bar and date widget read from `world.day` and the current block (e.g. Morning 08:xx, Afternoon 14:xx, Evening 20:xx), using `Calendar.format_day`.
- **Rent day.** Move "Rent is due Friday" to "Rent's due Monday", or move the bill to Friday. It's a one-line data change either way.

## 20. P3: Poverty teeth (ideas)

The goal is that **being broke at the bedsit is a real, escalating threat, with a way out that costs you**. Ranked by how well each fits the existing systems:

1. **The arrears ladder (recommended core).** Arrears weeks already exist in `TimeSystem`; give each week a consequence at the bedsit:
   - **Week 1:** a landlord letter (Notifications + an event card) and 5% interest (exists).
   - **Week 2:** "Bailiffs". On Monday rollover, lose the highest-value stash item or 25% of stored ore. One event card, with a pre-emptive choice to hide some of it (a roll, via the P1 check).
   - **Week 3:** "Locked out". You lose access to the HQ (no lab, no rest heal; rest becomes "sofa at Archie's", healing half) until the arrears are cleared plus a deposit (one week's rent). The HQ tab shows a padlocked door plate.
2. **Archie fronts you (the release valve).** When arrears first land, Archie offers to cover the rent. The debt is repaid automatically by shifting his sales split from 50/50 to 60/40 his way until it's cleared. It's a real cost with no hard fail, and in character ("Mates' rates. Well — rates.").
3. **Firm credit (the dark option).** If you refuse Archie or owe too much, a Firm member offers a loan at brutal interest. Missing a repayment triggers a debt-collector street encounter: a forced fight or pay. This gives the Firm an early, personal hook before Collective Act 1 makes them antagonists.
4. **Phone cut off (diegetic).** The phone bill (a small weekly cost) goes unpaid, and the phone is "out of credit" for a day: Messages, BizBrief offers and LodedInnit are greyed, and only the bank and Settings work. It's cheap to build, very on-theme, and it hurts exactly the management loop.
5. **"Skint" status.** While cash is under a threshold (e.g. £20) at rollover: rest heals 50%, and Archie's mugging risk on sales rises (desperate deals). It clears when cash recovers.
6. **Desperation work (the positive side).** While in arrears, rare shady job offers appear: carry a package, sit on a vein overnight. They pay well and have a roll for trouble. Poverty creates opportunities as well as punishments.
7. **Pawn shop.** The Trade sheet gains a "pawn" lane: instant cash at 50% of market, buy back within 7 days at 70%.

**Recommended package:** 1 + 2 + 4. The ladder gives the teeth, Archie's loan gives the escape at a price, and the phone cut-off gives flavour. Then 3 (Firm credit) as the first Act 2 hook. These need a numbers pass in REFERENCE §3.3 and an amendment to ADR 0006 (which currently makes arrears a no-op at the bedsit).

## 21. P4: BizBrief as the daily "what now"

### 21.1 The problem today
- After Rest, the Brief opens on accounts (Closing / Reynard's, Net change), then "Needs your attention", Treasury and Operations feed.
- "Needs your attention" (`MorningAccountsSystem.attention_items()`) only knows about alarms, guard shortfalls, development-eligible veins and unread messages.
- It ignores the things that actually decide a good day: quest objectives, veins drifting toward collapse or Rampant, contracts short of goods, offers expiring, rent/wages due, market spikes on items you hold, and staff blocked by no ore.

### 21.2 Proposal: a "Today" card at the top of the Brief

```

│ TODAY · TUE 2 APR · 3 blocks left  ●●●   │
├──────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ▲ URGENT                                 │
```

**How it works:**
- **New system** `systems/daily_brief.gd` (static, pure, testable) returns ranked items `{tier, kind, label, consequence, action}`.
- **Four tiers, in fixed order:**
  - **Urgent:** something bad happens if you do nothing within 1–2 days. Alarms, rent or wage shortfall, a vein at Barren/Spent or Rampant, a contract period about to fail, a guard shortfall deadline.
  - **Story:** the current active objective from `Objectives`/`Todo` (one line, the same text as ToDo).
  - **Opportunity:** offers expiring within 1 day, Ticker demand ≥ +25% on goods you hold, a faction favour asked.
  - **Routine:** veins ready to prune, idle staff, production short of ore. Collapsed to one summary line.
- **Every row has one consequence line and one action button** that deep-links (the Map vein panel, the Trade sheet, the Messages thread, the Sales offer). Deep links reuse existing nav: `PhoneNav`, `MapNav`, `Modal`.
- **Capped at about 6 visible rows.** The rest sits behind "+N more". The empty state reads "Nothing pressing. A good day to prospect." (prose TBD), which also fixes the "nothing pressing" ToDo complaint in `bugs-28-08-26.txt`.
- **Opens after every Rest,** as now, scrolled to Today.
- **The Phone home BizBrief badge** = Urgent + Story count only, so the badge means "you should look".
- **Block pips** (●●○) show remaining time blocks, tying the day structure to the plan.
- **"Done today" ticks.** An acted-on item ticks for the rest of the day (session state), so the list feels like progress.

**Not in scope:** auto-play or "do it for me" buttons. The Brief points; the player acts.

## 24. P8: What "expand the district deck" means

**Today:**
- 19 deck events in total across 13 districts. Each district has **one** card of its own (the City has two, **Shoreditch has none**), plus 7 "any district" cards shared everywhere.
- 13 of 19 have **no choice**; several are 1–2 cards long.
- Every entry has weight 1, and `barometerState` is unused (all `null`).

So a player working one district sees the same ~8 cards cycle almost immediately, and most of them ask nothing of the player.

**What I mean by "triple it" (~60 cards):**
- **3–4 cards per district** (about 45), plus about 15 "any" cards. Each district then has a recognisable personality: Camden hustle, City surveillance, Hampstead old money.
- **At least 2/3 of the cards carry a decision**, priced in the game's currencies (time block, cash, ore, relation, risk). The new P1 `check` system gives them rolls with visible odds. Example: *King's Cross delay*. Wait (lose a block) · cab (£30) · "jump the barrier" (roll 50%, +20% with Enhancement Powder; fail = £80 fine).
- **Use the existing hooks:**
  - `barometerState`: e.g. 2–3 cards that only appear during "crime" or "boom" Ticker states, so the news is felt on the street.
  - `excludeIfFlag` / a new requires-flag: cards that change after story beats (Firm cards appear once you're on their radar).
  - Higher weights, used to make rarer, bigger cards feel special.
- **Some cards chain:** a card sets a flag that unlocks a follow-up in the same district days later. This gives cheap mini-stories, and London builds opinions over time.
- **Keep the good vignettes** (foxes, conclave_watch, roman_brick). They become the low-weight texture layer, not the bulk.

That's about 40 new short events (3–5 cards each). It's mostly a content task once P1's check schema exists.
