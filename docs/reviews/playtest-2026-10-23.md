# Vein — Playtest Review

2026-10-23 · fresh-start playtest, ~1 hr, 390×844 portrait

## Summary

**Verdict: the writing and art are ship-quality; the game around them isn't yet playable on a phone.** In about an hour from New Game I reached the first crafting lesson and got stuck. Along the way the game crashed three times, lost a full run because there's no autosave, and every story button sat off-screen.

**Top 3 strengths**

1. **Voice.** Archie, James and Nick are distinct within two lines each. The jokes land dry ("Bit more of a Mr Bean vibe"; "Make of that what you will about humanity").
2. **Art direction.** The rain-soaked pixel London, the Wetherspoons, James's workshop and the craft bench are all striking, and consistent across scenes.
3. **Hooks.** Rent due Monday, a pearl that rewinds time, and calc that *moves toward you*. That's three layers of motivation set up in under 20 minutes.

**Top 3 problems**

1. **Story cards overflow the screen.** On the 390×844 target the text box and every → / choice button sit at y 780–1016. Without tapping off-screen a phone player can't advance past card 1.
2. **Stability and saves.** Opening Archie's day-2 thread crashed 3/3 times (fixed during the session). With no autosave, the crash cost the whole prologue.
3. **Crafting is a wall.** The first lesson needs rank 2, but the visible green target doesn't match the score. Feedback is hidden below a scroll, and there's no way out of the bench.

## What works

**Writing.** It's dry, specific and British without overdoing it. Each line does one job. Standouts:

- "You have about four questions. He looks good for one." This sets the limits of the conversation through character instead of UI.
- James: "I'm the only craftsman you know. There's a difference." His whole character in one line.
- "He examines the calc with the careful enthusiasm of someone who has recently read about it online."

**The opening hook.** Rent due Monday + £40 + a dodgy mate gives instant stakes with zero fantasy jargon. The fantasy arrives through an object (a warm hourglass), not exposition.

**Rewind as the flagship moment.** The tutorial fight is unwinnable (Nick hits for 120; you do 7). It funnels you into breaking the hourglass, and "Time unspools. Only you remember" is a genuine wow beat.

**Combat readability.** Four moves (Guard / Strike / Feint / Stun) plus a visible enemy intent card make a clear rock-paper-scissors that a new player can read in one turn. On the failed escape, Archie yelling "Break it. Now!" is a strong contextual nudge.

**The phone as hub.** Phone / Map / HQ tabs and a home screen of apps suit a contemporary London setting. BizBrief's "Today" card (story step, rent progress, opportunity, blocks left) is the best UI in the build. It answers "what do I do next?" every time.

**Branching that respects you.** Asking for half gets "Sixty-forty, then. Your way." Different pub questions give different lore. Small, but it shows the system can carry real choice.

**Art.** It's consistent, atmospheric pixel art with real place-specificity (Wetherspoons carpet, Shoreditch neon, Bermondsey wet paving). The craft bench, with labelled ore jars and a ring-bound recipe book, makes a mechanic feel physical.

### Continuity slips

- Shoreditch card says "The next morning", but the art is a night bar and Archie said "tonight".
- Archie says James is "tomorrow, midday"; the card reads "This morning — Bermondsey".
- James: "That hourglass **Archie** broke". The player broke it.
- The pub art doesn't change for "Four streets later — a closed launderette" (still the alley).
- Run 2 combat header says "Archie's down" while Archie is up and Guarded.
- The HQ wall calendar reads "Oct" in April.

### Design friction

- **False choices.** Archie: "no hard feelings if it's a no", then the game says "I'll come" for you. "Your call. Stay or come", then it picks "I'll stay". Three times in the first hour, the narration asks for a decision and then makes it.
- **The prologue plays itself.** After Rewind, the log shows "You get your guard up… You leg it" without input. The flagship moment is watched, not done.
- **Rewind is over-explained.** Item reads "Rewind — tier 5 (to the start)" in the same scene where Archie "doesn't say what it does". It's also called an hourglass, a Time Pearl and Rewind within five minutes.
- **Passive economy beats.** The first sale, the first cash, and rent progress all happen in narration. The player never sets a price, picks a buyer or takes a risk.
- **Opaque crafting.** Per-step feedback ("~ more pressure", "~ hotter") is hidden below a scroll. The meaning of a score of 0 or 4 isn't explained, and the lesson's rank-2 bar has no visible threshold.
- **The ticker.** "RESTED. WED 2 APR. 0 HP." reads as "you have 0 HP". The split-flap animation looks like garbled text for the first second.
- **Onboarding chrome.** The character select has no labels or text. The title screen is a grey void with "Debug Start" visible. Eleven of the twelve apps are unexplained on day 1.

## POV: mobile casual-mid player

**Would churn in session 1, most likely on card 1.** On a real phone the → button is off-screen, so this player can't get past the first card of the game.

If that were fixed:

- **Minutes 0–15 hook well.** Short cards, punchy lines and a clear goal (rent) fit commute-length play.
- **Session length is too front-loaded.** The prologue is about 20 taps of reading before the first input that matters. Casual players want a tap-that-does-something inside the first minute.
- **Phone hub is friendly but crowded.** BizBrief's "do this next" card is exactly right. Twelve app icons on day 1, most of them empty, is noise.
- **There's no idle or return reward yet.** Rest = next morning, with no "while you were away" payoff. Morning report reads "No payday or operations".
- **The crafting minigame is the right genre** (tactile, short, repeatable). But failing three times with a hidden reason, and no way out, is a hard quit.
- **Losing progress to a crash** with no autosave is the single most churn-inducing thing in the build.

## POV: core strategy / sim player

**Intrigued by the frame, starved of decisions.** In an hour I made about four meaningful choices, and none of them were economic.

- **The economy hasn't started yet.** I saw cash go from £40 to £80, a £350 rent target, five ore types and "3 blocks left". I never chose what to buy, sell, price or where to spend a block. BizBrief's Manage tab and "Business pot not open yet" promise depth that isn't reachable in the first hour.
- **The rent deadline is a great pressure clock**, but there's no visible plan to hit it. How many blocks until Monday? What earns money? A strategy player wants the math on day 1.
- **Combat has a real core.** Intents + Guard/Strike/Feint/Stun + party targeting is enough to build on. The tutorial just never lets you use it.
- **Crafting depth is promising but illegible.** Ranks, XP, failed-run counters and "Prophet's Breath" / "Be a Lady" modifiers suggest an optimisation layer. A min-maxer needs the target band to match the score, and numeric feedback per step (e.g. "pressure 58, target 64–76").
- **Time blocks aren't enforced** (the Shoreditch event was free), which undermines trust in the resource system early.

## POV: narrative-first player

**This is the audience the build already serves best.** I'd keep reading for Archie and James alone.

- **The characters are distinct immediately.** Archie is a warm, cowardly fixer with an allergy joke that pays off. James is precise and contemptuous, with something hidden. Nick is menace in four words ("New one. Lovely.").
- **The mystery is seeded well.** The calc drifts toward you, "one person in a few thousand", "not even Archie". That's exactly enough to want the next chapter.
- **Grounded London specificity** (Tesco bag, Pret, Poundland in Stratford, Costcutter) sells the "same city, different rules" tagline.
- **The false choices hurt this player most.** Being asked "your call" and then having the game answer for you breaks the player's role. Either cut the question or honour both answers, even if they converge a card later.
- **Continuity slips are noticeable** to an attentive reader (who broke the hourglass, morning vs night, tomorrow vs this morning). Each is small; together they make the world feel less real.
- **Pacing.** The Shoreditch sale is roughly ten cards of no-choice chat. Trim it, or give one beat of agency (read Marcus, haggle, notice something).

## Prioritised recommendations

Fix the first three before any further playtest with real players; everything after is ordered by impact per effort.

1. **Fit story cards to the viewport.** Anchor the card to the bottom of the safe area, cap the art height, and keep → and choices always on-screen.
2. **Autosave** at every event end and day change. Commit the `messages_app.gd` crash fix and add a regression test for leaving a thread mid-reveal.
3. **Make crafting passable and legible.** Align the crank's green zone with the scoring band. Show per-step results inline ("58 — needed 64–76"). Put feedback above the fold, and add a Leave button to the bench.
4. **Honour or cut every "your call".** Give the three false choices real buttons, even if the branches reconvene.
5. **Let the player do the Rewind.** After breaking the pearl, hand control back for one turn (Guard → Leg it) instead of auto-resolving.
6. **Give the first sale one decision**: price, buyer read, or haggle. It's the economy's first beat and should be played.
7. **Fix the continuity slips** listed above (one pass, ~6 lines of copy and 1 art swap).
8. **Day-1 phone diet.** Hide or lock apps until relevant, and badge only the next step. Make the ticker read "Rested · HP full".
9. **Show the rent plan.** In BizBrief, add "Days left · blocks left · £ needed" so the strategy player can plan from day 1.
10. **Title and character select.** Add a key-art background and hide Debug Start in release builds. Put a name or one-line hook on each character option.
