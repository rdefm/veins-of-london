# VEIN — Character and voice guide

Draft for prose review, based on the current event copy and [CONTENT-GUIDE.md](CONTENT-GUIDE.md), including current working edits (20 September 2026).
Personality and voice guidance combine the existing portrayal with the author's direction on Nadia and Des's Windrush backgrounds and views of community.
Each character has eight sentences, covering personality, relevant context, and voice.

## Archie

**Personality:** Blunt, quick-witted and commercially minded, Archie makes generosity look like a sensible business arrangement.
He is capable under pressure and protective of the player, with concern showing through practical help and brief moments of unguarded sincerity.

**Context:** An old friend who brings the player into the calc trade, he finds buyers, introduces James and Des, and becomes the player's partner after the home raid.
His time allergy is explicit canon in the prose guide, while his grievance against Des centres on an unpaid £420 that Des considers settled, exposing their different ideas about obligation.

**Voice:** Use cockney-inflected conversational English, contractions, “calc”, brisk instructions and sharp observations about money, inconvenience and other people's competence.
Let advice arrive wrapped in a dig, but make the advice useful and the danger real.
When someone is hurt, shorten the patter and allow a direct question about whether they are actually all right.
Keep affection in what he brings, arranges or gives away, and let him cut the conversation short before gratitude becomes uncomfortable.

## James

**Personality:** Brilliant, bitter and exacting, James regards other people's ignorance as an imposition on his time.
His reluctant generosity shows in the instruction and equipment he provides, despite his insistence that helping is merely expedient.

**Context:** A craftsman in his sixties working from a Bermondsey storage unit, he teaches the player time pearls and enhancement powder before offering overflow work.
The first meeting hints that he notices something unusual about the player, but leaves what he recognises unexplained.

**Voice:** Use precise, controlled sentences, exact corrections and insults aimed at a specific failure of knowledge or competence.
His references to Plato and the Critias express expertise and irritation at the present state of the trade, so keep them attached to the subject at hand.
Let pauses, qualifications and narrowly conceded approval reveal more than overt encouragement; he insists on defining the player as an assistant and controlling the terms.
Keep insults predominantly non-crude, permit at most one calibrated profanity per scene, and make the practical instruction underneath the contempt clear.

## Des

**Personality:** Desmond, usually Des, is second-generation Windrush, an unhurried man in his forties whose warmth and enthusiasm for ground are unusually open in this company.
He holds an idealistic view of how a community should operate in hard times: people share what they have, protect one another's privacy and meet need without keeping a personal ledger.

**Context:** Archie introduces him as the best prospector he knows, and Des teaches the player prospecting and seeding before drawing them into the Collective through useful work.
He regards his old business with Archie as resolved, while the attacks on Collective members expose both his protective seriousness and his caution about measures that could become habitual.

**Voice:** Use relaxed London speech shaped by his second-generation Windrush background, patient repetition and concrete examples of people helping one another through hard times.
Keep his humour mild and let his idealism sound like an expectation of ordinary decency, with brief statements of principle followed by practical help.
He normally writes full sentences in messages, so fragmented texts and unusual silence should register as signs of distress.
Let him be firm about mutual responsibility while leaving people room to choose, with generosity expressed through coffee, shared calc and time spent teaching.

## Nadia

**Personality:** Nadia Beckford is of the Windrush generation, about seventy, physically capable, fiercely practical and confident in her own competence.
She knows from lived experience that community helps people overcome hardship, and puts that knowledge into practice through fair dealing, mutual support and durable solutions.

**Context:** Based in Hackney, she moves substantial quantities of calc for the Collective, but its lack of shared ownership records makes filling her longstanding time-calc order laborious.
Her dealings with the player progress from a supply request to buying a producing vein, and she later pushes for action when the Firm's intimidation becomes a pattern.

**Voice:** Let her language reflect her Windrush-generation Caribbean heritage and years in London through natural cadence and idiom, using short, declarative sentences that name the problem, the terms and the next useful action.
Give her the vocabulary of an experienced trader—orders, amounts, prices, ownership and time wasted—with a dry impatience for avoidable inefficiency.
Let her authority on community come from experience, expressed through concrete advice and specific offers of help, with Caribbean inflection carried by phrasing rather than exaggerated phonetic spelling.
Under threat, keep her decisive and pragmatic, able to accept another person's objection provisionally without sounding persuaded for good.

## Hakim

**Personality:** Hakim is modest, conscientious and easily embarrassed by needing help, with pride in his shop and loyalty to his uncle.
He habitually minimises his own importance, yet refuses to sell under intimidation and shows quiet courage when that refusal gets him hurt.

**Context:** A Whitechapel Road newsagent, he struggles to maintain the yard vein his uncle once tended, whose declining output matters to the rent.
After the player restores it, he shares local leads overheard in the shop and later identifies life ground that helps the player establish another vein.

**Voice:** Use everyday shopkeeper language, hesitant qualifications, self-corrections and sentences that trail off as he approaches a complaint or an admission of need.
Let embarrassment interrupt his thanks and offers of repayment without obscuring what he is trying to say.
His humour comes from ordinary circumstances and awkward phrasing; in the attack aftermath, his understatement carries the hurt without making the violence a joke.
Keep his local observations useful and specific, while he insists he is merely someone behind a counter who hears people talk.

## Shared writing rule

Keep danger sincere, magic an ordinary commodity, and humour rooted in coping with practical circumstances; the narrator's dry observations should not make every character sound equally sardonic.
